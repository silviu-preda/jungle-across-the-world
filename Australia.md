# Australia

Australia is a high-load country for this project, so research is being conducted label-first and then expanded through artists, releases, archived forums, radio tracklists, tape scans, and scene documentation. This is an initial snapshot rather than a claim of completeness.

The entries below follow the country-allocation guardrail: artists count through qualifying activity in the Australian scene even when they were born elsewhere or released through foreign labels. A foreign label remains attached to its own scene.

## Artists

### Syndicate

**Relevant jungle activity:** 1990s  
**Location:** Newcastle / Bloody Fist orbit

Syndicate belongs in the Australian story through the Newcastle Bloody Fist ecosystem. The project's listening review confirmed the group's jungle relevance rather than treating the connection as a general hardcore association.

### 12bit Jungle Out There

**Relevant jungle activity:** Contemporary  
**Location:** Australia

12bit Jungle Out There is a direct Australian jungle entry. The project's listening review found the material clearly within scope; the Amiga/12-bit production identity is part of the project's character rather than a reason to file it only as breakcore.

### BUEX

**Relevant jungle activity:** Contemporary  
**Location:** Perth-associated Australian scene

BUEX remains relevant to Australia even when releases appear on overseas labels. *Form* was released by the UK label Omni Music, while *Eat the Bangers Vol. 1* supplied the stronger listening case for inclusion. This is a useful example of why artist geography and label geography must not be collapsed into one another.

### Greg Packer

**Relevant jungle activity:** 1990s–present  
**Location:** Perth

Greg Packer is a substantial historical and continuing Australian jungle/drum-and-bass figure. The strongest production anchor found in this pass is *Terrorschizm (Jungle Mix)* with MC Assassin, which the listening review found unequivocally effective. *Gunshot Riddim* leans further toward drum and bass, and later work is generally less decisive for this project's narrower purpose. His 1990s DJ recordings are valuable evidence of early local jungle circulation, but mixes are kept distinct from his original productions.

Packer's Interphase imprint is relevant as a Perth scene node, but is retained as a brief contextual mention until its catalogue has been documented more fully.

### Time Travel

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2000s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne

Time Travel is part of the Melbourne network around Rising Lantern, Bitwise and Echo Chamber Sound. The connection is strongest through locally embedded label and collaborative activity; the separate Anomaly Digital connection is weaker and should be treated as supporting context rather than the main case.

### MshCode

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2000s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne

MshCode co-runs Rising Lantern with Time Travel. *Silver Dub – The Remixes* was a strong listening result in this research pass and helps establish the junction between Melbourne dubwise production and jungle/breaks practice.

### LQ

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2010s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne-based

LQ is a New Zealand-born producer, DJ, label operator and soundsystem figure whose sustained Melbourne activity places him in the Australian scene under the project's allocation rules. Echo Chamber Sound and Run It Red provide the central local infrastructure, and the listening review confirmed that his catalogue contains definite jungle rather than merely adjacent dub or drum and bass.

### Kloke

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2010s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne-based

Kloke is British-born and Melbourne-based. His jungle work is substantial, including collaborations with Tim Reaper, and the listening review marked him as a major Australian-scene entry. Birthplace does not override the scene in which the qualifying activity is created and sustained.

### Dizz1

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2010s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne

Dizz1 qualifies through Melbourne activity and releases such as *In the Zone EP*, whose palette includes explicit junglist work alongside a broader bass-music practice.

### Tactical Aspect

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2010s  
**Location:** East-coast Australia

Tactical Aspect is the duo of DJ Speaks and Ruff Tactics. The project is firmly jungle, while DJ Speaks also supplies an important bridge back to Sydney's 1990s Jungle Massive Australia history. The Australian scene connection is operational rather than dependent on birthplace.

### Paulblackout

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2000s–present  
**Location:** Newcastle / Australian hardcore-breaks scene

Paulblackout is central to the later Hardline Rekordingz story. The catalogue moves across hardcore, drum and bass, breakcore and jungle, but releases including *Hardline 13 – Viva La Revolucion* sit productively at the junction and were accepted in the listening review. *Drum Embrace* adds a worthwhile IDM/breaks fusion. *Breakology* is deliberately excluded from the current selection.

### Arkon

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2000s  
**Location:** Perth-associated Australian scene

Arkon enters through the Hardline circle and direct Amen-led material. *Rapture* was the clearest listening anchor in this pass. The historical Australian connection is retained even where later expatriate activity complicates a one-city label.

### Pugilist

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2010s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne-based

Pugilist is a Scottish/Kiwi producer operating from Melbourne and therefore belongs to the Australian scene under the project's location rule. He is stylistically versatile, so his jungle material is scattered rather than confined to a single label or phase.

The strongest entry found so far is *Waveforms 19–20*: both tracks use mid-1990s break architecture, with *Waveform 20* providing a particularly direct Amen workout. *Onyx* is another major result. *Looking Out* is good but sits slightly outside pure jungle, while the Pugilist & Tamen *Source* EP establishes a further jungle lane through the cross-border Ruff Kutz imprint.

### Tokyo Noir

**Relevant jungle activity:** 2010s  
**Location:** Melbourne

Tokyo Noir is a Melbourne producer with releases across Australian and Australian-origin bass labels. The three-track *Dopamine* release appeared on Detrimental Audio in 2017 and contains the title track, *Video Nasty*, and a Token remix of *Dopamine*. His Inna Riddim material remains an active listening lead rather than a finished catalogue judgment.

## Labels

### Bloody Fist Records

**Relevant activity:** 1994–2004, with archival reissues afterward  
**Location:** Newcastle

Bloody Fist qualifies outright. Although famous for an abrasive mixture of hardcore, speedcore, breakcore and industrial extremity, it also contains numerous releases that meet this project's jungle threshold. Syndicate is a direct historical entry; Epsilon's *Christ as Bath Toy* is another useful catalogue marker. Bloody Fist is not being included merely as adjacent context: its jungle-bearing catalogue is part of the Australian story.

### Bitwise

**Relevant activity:** 2010s–present  
**Location:** Melbourne

Bitwise is one of the strongest discoveries in this research round. Confirmed listening highlights include *Silver Dub – The Remixes*, Subnet43's *The Empirical EP*, and especially *The Mutant Limit EP*, whose clean, energetic Amen chopping invited comparison with Tim Reaper. Time Travel is also relevant to the catalogue. Artist-by-artist provenance still needs to be completed before every Bitwise contributor is allocated to Australia individually.

### Rising Lantern Records

**Relevant activity:** 2008–present  
**Location:** Melbourne

Rising Lantern is run by brothers MshCode and Time Travel and is an important route into Melbourne's dubwise jungle network. The catalogue and roster lead through Subnet43, OneSec, Rich Tones, Visible Sound, Stemfilth, SpinFX, C:1, Strangenotes and Civilian Flexibility, among others.

The project's listening review found Rich Tones especially strong. Stemfilth also has an individual trail through *Intensity* and *Three Spots*. Civilian Flexibility appears in the *Dub Plate Specials* series. Visible Sound is retained as an artist lead, but the proposed identification with Alistair Brown is not treated as established.

### Echo Chamber Sound / Run It Red

**Relevant activity:** 2008–present  
**Location:** Melbourne

Echo Chamber Sound is a Melbourne label, record shop and soundsystem institution with New Zealand roots and a deliberately trans-Tasman catalogue. It supports Australian-scene entries including LQ, Kloke and Dizz1 while also releasing New Zealand artists who must remain allocated to New Zealand.

Run It Red, its jungle-focused subsidiary, is the clearest genre-specific arm and qualifies directly. Catalogued work by Zeldin supplied another listening-positive jungle result, while Shadow Club and Midnight Dubs are useful cross-border catalogue evidence rather than Australian artist allocations. Sekkleman remains predominantly dub for this project's purposes.

### Hardline Rekordingz / Hardline Rekz Digital

**Relevant activity:** 2000s–present  
**Location:** Australia

Hardline belongs in the project through a catalogue that repeatedly crosses hardcore, breakcore, drum and bass and jungle. Important listening results include *The Subtle Arts of Murder & Persuasion* as a series—Part 1 is the stronger jungle document despite the distorted, poor-quality Bandcamp master—Arkon's *Rapture*, *Broke / Mindjack VIP*, *Hardline 13 – Viva La Revolucion*, and *Drum Embrace*. *Breakology* is excluded from the selected group.

### Detrimental Audio

**Relevant activity:** 2010s–present  
**Location:** Established in Melbourne; subsequently based in London

Detrimental Audio is an Australian-origin label whose later London base should be stated rather than erased. Its catalogue offers genuine jungle routes through artists including Tactical Aspect, Tokyo Noir and Pickleman. Tokyo Noir's *Dopamine* is a documented Australian-artist release; Pickleman's music is a strong listening result, but his own national/scene allocation remains unresolved.

### ENDE Records Australia

**Relevant activity:** 2014–present  
**Location:** Perth

ENDE is predominantly a breakcore/hardcore/IDM/noise label, but its catalogue contains enough genuine jungle to qualify at label level. Enja Bergman's *Atmospheric Jungle* is not merely atmospheric music with breakbeats: the listening review found Amen jungle throughout. 3 Past 3 is another strong jungle/breakcore result. Both artists remain allocated outside Australia where their own provenance requires it; they are evidence for the Australian label, not automatically Australian artists.

### 8-Bit Recordings

**Relevant activity:** 2000s–present  
**Location:** Perth

Founded by Noistruct, The Last Ninja and Deadcode, 8-Bit is mainly a breakcore, glitch, IDM and noise institution. It receives a contextual label entry rather than a claim that the whole catalogue is jungle. Holt's *Nocturne* is the likeliest qualifying track found so far, while Deadcode's *The Dirty Truth* contains worthwhile chopped-break material.

### System Corrupt / SY:CO

**Relevant activity:** 2000s  
**Location:** Australian underground network

System Corrupt is retained as a contextual scene entry. Its centre of gravity is breakcore, noise, speedcore and hardcore, with occasional jungle fusion. It should not be rewritten as a jungle label, but it matters to the porous Australian ecosystem this project is documenting.

### Anomaly Digital

**Relevant activity:** 2010s  
**Location:** Australia

Anomaly Digital has a future-jungle/hardcore-breaks remit, but the catalogue proved less decisive in listening than its descriptions suggested. Kranky & Lethal were too tangential for individual inclusion. A Time Travel connection gives the label a limited supporting role and warrants a footnote-level entry rather than flagship status.

### Golden Orb Records

**Relevant activity:** 2010s  
**Location:** Brisbane

Golden Orb is broadly an atmospheric/liquid drum-and-bass label rather than a jungle specialist. It qualifies narrowly through Shebuzzz's *In Infinity*, whose aggressive Amen drop passed the listening test. The artist's own country allocation remains unresolved, and several other sampled Golden Orb releases did not make the cut.

### Interphase

**Relevant activity:** Historical  
**Location:** Perth

Greg Packer's Interphase is included as a concise historical label mention. Its Perth connection is clear, but this pass did not establish enough accessible, full-length catalogue evidence for a larger profile.

## Collectives and historical infrastructure

### Jungle Massive Australia

**Relevant activity:** Mid-1990s onward  
**Location:** Sydney

Jungle Massive Australia (JMA) is an essential historical collective and scene institution. DJ Popeye's *Jungle Massive Australia Vol. 1* (1995) and *This Is Jungle: JMA Vol. II* (1996) are strong period documents. They are DJ tapes/mixes rather than evidence of original production, and are described accordingly.

DJ Speaks was an early JMA figure and later one half of Tactical Aspect. This continuity links the formative Sydney jungle network to later Australian production and performance activity. Archived forum material, surviving tracklists and uploaded tape recordings are especially important here because conventional streaming metadata is incomplete.

## Research state and unresolved leads

The following findings are preserved so that the next pass starts from the actual listening work rather than repeating it:

- **Big Dog Records / Adelaide:** *MPK Presents: Archive – A Collection of Original Adelaide Drum & Bass* has been located as a downloadable archive. Full listening is deferred until the files can be auditioned at home; the label is not yet promoted to the confirmed list.
- **Inna Riddim / Sydney:** a broad bass label and active route into Tokyo Noir. *The Feelin' / Keep the Vibes Alive* is still awaiting a final ear verdict.
- **B&H catalogue:** *BotB 2* was found jungle-focused; Nickname's *Groove 4* has an old-school jungle sound; *Deep into the Rave* is substantially jungle, including Noneohone's *CARLISLE* and MOOD$' *Forgotten*; SAV/Blank's *Dune Rider* on *Lucid Encounters* is liquid jungle; Knuckle's *Trick* and Myledo's *Freddy No* also passed listening. The exact label identity and Australian provenance need to be resolved before allocation.
- **XTR catalogue:** XTR001 did not qualify. Knuckle's *Manifold* from *Vamos EP* is definite jungle, *Trick* from *Bag of Tricks* also works, and Oi Ocha's *Jungle Informer EP* is relevant. Possible People's material was judged drum and bass. Label identity and artist provenance still need clean documentation.
- **Rising Lantern provenance:** Rich Tones, Stemfilth, Visible Sound and Civilian Flexibility remain worthwhile individual follow-ups. Visible Sound must not be identified as Alistair Brown without better evidence.
- **Bitwise provenance:** *The Mutant Limit EP* and Subnet43's *The Empirical EP* are firm musical discoveries; individual Australian allocation still needs independent support.
- **Best Effort:** not a jungle label. Nutcase & Papachubba and a Hugh B release justify only light artist-level follow-up.
- **Xian:** an important Melbourne breakcore pioneer, but the reviewed tracks were not pure jungle. *Cold Crash Breakcore* came closest, followed by *Dem*; retain as contextual breakcore history rather than a core jungle entry.
- **Atom 1 / Soundboy Melbourne:** *Imminent* is historical Melbourne techstep/drum and bass, not a jungle anchor.
- **Excluded or deprioritised in this pass:** Royalston, Evil Eye Recordings, CC:White, Breakology, Possible People and Sekkleman.
